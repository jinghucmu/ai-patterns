## Where the patterns come from

I do not collect patterns from other people's products on this site. Each one is drawn from work I led or contributed to as a product designer, and each pattern page names the product it came from. My reviews of other companies' AI products live on a separate site, [AI product design reviews](https://jinghucmu.github.io/ai-design-patterns/).

Because the source is my own work, I can say things a survey of products cannot: what the real difficulty was, which part of the problem turned out to be easy, and how the design behaves when the AI gets it wrong.

## What a pattern page contains

Every pattern follows the same outline, so that two patterns can be compared:

- **The problem.** The situation a person is in, in plain words.
- **The pattern.** What the design does about it.
- **What makes it hard.** The tension that makes this a design problem and not an engineering one.
- **When it fails.** What the person experiences when the AI is wrong, and what the design does about that.
- **The accessibility dividend.** What the same structure gives to people who use the product without sound, without pictures or without a mouse. Not every pattern has one yet.
- **In my work.** The product the pattern came from.

Each page also shows which stage of the flow it serves: ask, answer, check or decide. The flow itself is a pattern, [Ask, answer, check, decide](../patterns/ask-answer-check-decide/).

## The three themes

Patterns are grouped into three themes that match the three areas my work has covered: AI in shopping, AI across modes, and AI for accessibility. Most patterns sit in more than one. The first group, written in October 2026, all come from video shopping and so all sit in the multimodal theme; the other two themes fill in as I write up more work.

## Pictures

Where a pattern has screenshots, they are of the product as it shipped, and every image has a text description. Patterns without screenshots are not less real; I add pictures when I have ones I can share.
