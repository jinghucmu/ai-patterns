## What users needed

The moment a person wants to act on something happens inside the content, while they are still looking at it. What they needed was to act in that moment. Leaving the content to search, remember and compare broke the moment, and most of the time the action went with it. They also made clear what they did not want: anything laid over the content that looked like it was selling to them.

## The problem

Someone is looking at a recording, an image or a document, and sees the thing they need: a device in a frame, a term in a scan, a figure in a report. That is the moment they want to act on it. But most content is a display surface. To get from what they see to what they can do about it, they have to leave the content, remember what they saw, and go looking for it. Most of the time they do not.

## The pattern

When AI recognizes that something in the content is a thing a person can act on, mark it inside the content itself, at the place and moment it appears. One tap on the marker does the thing: opens the detail, saves it, adds it, flags it for someone.

The content stops being a picture of things and becomes a surface of things. What the eye sees can be acted on where it is.

## What makes it hard

Two things, and neither is the recognition.

**Trust.** The marker has to be accurate, but it cannot be loud. Visually it must not read as a promotion or an interruption, and it must not pop up at a cut and break the rhythm of attention. A marker that feels like an intrusion costs more trust than it earns.

**Timing.** In content that moves, markers have to appear and disappear in step with where the eye already is. The person should never be pulled in two directions in the same second: one toward what the content is showing, the other toward a button that just arrived.

## When it fails

If the marker is on the wrong thing, the person learns to ignore every later marker. If it arrives a beat early or late, it competes with the content instead of extending it. Both failures are quiet; nobody complains, they just stop tapping.

## The accessibility dividend

A marker is also a fact about the content that does not depend on the picture: at this second, this thing is on screen. Once that fact exists, it can be read aloud, listed, or reached with a keyboard. See [Structure is accessibility](../structure-is-accessibility/).

## In my work

Video Hotspots are the buttons that grow inside shopping videos on Amazon: when the AI recognizes a product on screen, a marker appears on it at that second, and one tap opens the product or adds it to the cart. I designed how they look, when they appear and how they get out of the way.
