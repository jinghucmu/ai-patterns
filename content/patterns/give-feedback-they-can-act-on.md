## What users needed

Sellers were not video professionals, and they knew it. When the first version of a video came back, the most common reaction in research was not "this is wrong" but "I can tell something is off and I don't know what to do about it." They wanted what a small business cannot usually get: a second opinion from someone who makes videos for a living, delivered before the video goes out, in words they could act on.

## The problem

AI that produces work for people usually stops at producing it. Where it does comment, it tends to comment in general terms, "consider a stronger opening," "the text could be simpler," which reads as a grade and leaves the person exactly where they were: aware of a problem, unsure where it is or what to do. A review that cannot be acted on is a second piece of work handed to someone who asked for help with the first.

## The pattern

Have the AI review the work the way an experienced colleague would. Tie every comment to a place: this second, this frame, this line, this field. Say what is weak there and why it matters to the person's goal. Then offer the change as an action, applied to the work in front of them, with the current version kept until they choose. The person reads the comment where the problem is, sees what the fix would do, and decides.

Keep the review in the person's terms, not the model's. A seller wants to know that the opening is static and that a camera move will hold attention; they do not want a list of parameters.

## What makes it hard

The comment has to be right about the specific place it points to, or it is worse than no comment: a review that calls the wrong moment static teaches the person to ignore the next one. The suggested change has to be a real change the system can make, not a wish. And the review has to know when to stop. Three comments that matter are a colleague; fifteen are a checklist, and the person applies none of them.

## When it fails

If the feedback is generic, people stop opening it. If the fix is offered but applying it replaces the current version without asking, people lose work and stop trusting the review. If the review only ever finds fault and never says what is working, people read it as the system covering itself rather than helping them. And when the person's judgment and the AI's disagree, the person's must win without friction; the review is advice, not approval.

## The accessibility dividend

Feedback stated in words, anchored to a named place and offered as a labelled action, is feedback a screen reader can read in full. A review that depends on the person seeing what the AI saw in a frame is not.

## In my work

In the AI video creation tool for Amazon sellers, a seller who asks the assistant how to improve a video gets comments tied to specific moments: the opening is static and a camera move would help; the on-screen text is too long for the time it is shown; the final shot should hold longer. Each comment comes with the change as an action, the seller can apply it or keep the current version, and the revised video is shown next to the one it replaces. The aim was to give sellers the professional second opinion they could not otherwise get, before the video reached their customers.
