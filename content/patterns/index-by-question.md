## What users needed

Shoppers' questions were about using the product, not about the product's name: does it smoke, is it hard to clean, how much fits. They needed to get to the seconds that answered their own question, and they were impatient with any path through the video that was organized around the creator's priorities instead of theirs.

## The problem

Someone choosing an air fryer is not really asking "which air fryer". They are asking whether it is hard to use, whether it smokes, whether it holds enough food, what the food comes out like, and how annoying it is to clean. A review answers all of those, but scattered across thirty minutes in the order the creator happened to cook things.

## The pattern

Let AI organize the video by topic instead of by product: operating difficulty, smoke, capacity, food results, cleaning. Each topic becomes a place the shopper can jump to. The video is still the creator's, but the path through it belongs to the shopper.

This is the pattern I like most, because it comes closest to the person who scrolled into a thirty-minute review wanting only the three seconds when the wings come out of the basket.

## What makes it hard

[Index by product](../index-by-product/) leans on object recognition. This leans on something much harder: understanding what is being said and shown well enough to know that a stretch of video is about cleaning, even when nobody says the word. The topics have to be the shopper's words, not the model's, and the set of topics has to be short enough to scan and complete enough to trust.

## When it fails

A topic that is named in jargon, or that is really two topics, stops being a shortcut. Too many topics and the index is as long as the video. A topic that lands on the wrong seconds costs trust in all the others, so the same correction path matters here as in the product index.

## The accessibility dividend

A topic index is the closest thing a video has to headings. It lets a screen-reader user, a keyboard user or someone with limited attention move through a video the way they already move through a document. See [Structure is accessibility](../structure-is-accessibility/).

## In my work

Topic Jumplinks organize Amazon shopping videos by the questions shoppers bring to them. I designed how the topics are presented and how a shopper moves between them.
