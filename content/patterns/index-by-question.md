## What users needed

People's questions were about their own situation, not about the names of things: is it hard to use, what are the side effects, how much does it hold. They needed to get to the part that answered their own question, and they were impatient with any path through the content that was organized around the author's priorities instead of theirs.

## The problem

Someone choosing between options is not really asking "which one". They are asking whether it is hard to use, whether it has a problem they have heard about, whether it is enough for their case, what the result is like, and what the upkeep is. A long piece of content answers all of those, but scattered across thirty minutes or forty pages in the order the author happened to go through them.

## The pattern

Let AI organize the content by topic instead of by thing: ease of use, known problems, capacity, results, upkeep. Each topic becomes a place the person can jump to. The content is still the author's, but the path through it belongs to the reader.

This is the pattern I like most, because it comes closest to the person who came to a thirty-minute piece wanting only the three seconds that answer their question.

## What makes it hard

[Index by what it covers](../index-by-what-it-covers/) leans on recognizing things. This leans on something much harder: understanding what is being said and shown well enough to know that a stretch of content is about upkeep, even when nobody uses the word. The topics have to be the reader's words, not the model's, and the set of topics has to be short enough to scan and complete enough to trust.

## When it fails

A topic that is named in jargon, or that is really two topics, stops being a shortcut. Too many topics and the index is as long as the content. A topic that lands in the wrong place costs trust in all the others, so the same correction path matters here as in the index by what it covers.

## The accessibility dividend

A topic index is the closest thing a recording has to headings. It lets a screen-reader user, a keyboard user or someone with limited attention move through a recording the way they already move through a document. See [Structure is accessibility](../structure-is-accessibility/).

## In my work

Topic Jumplinks organize Amazon shopping videos by the questions people bring to them: for an air fryer review, how hard it is to use, whether it smokes, how much it holds, what the food comes out like, how annoying it is to clean. I designed how the topics are presented and how a person moves between them.
