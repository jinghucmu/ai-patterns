## What users needed

A large share of use happens without sound, on slow connections, by keyboard, or through a screen reader, and those people needed the same things a sighted user with headphones takes for granted: to know what is in the content, to get to the part they want, and to act on it. Nothing in a plain recording or image gave them that.

## The problem

Most people do not connect AI features with accessibility. Rich media is the least accessible material a product holds: a black box of pixels and sound that a screen reader cannot read, a keyboard cannot navigate and a slow connection cannot load. Accessibility work on it usually arrives after launch, as captions and a fix list.

## The pattern

Notice what every multimodal AI feature is doing underneath. Markers in the frame, an index by what it covers, an index by question and excerpt answers are all the same operation: they take information that was hidden inside the content and make it structured, readable and askable.

Once content has structure, it is naturally friendlier to screen readers, to silent use, to keyboard navigation, to low-bandwidth connections, to older users and to people with cognitive differences. So design the structure first, as the foundation of the product, and let both the AI features and the accessibility fall out of it.

## What makes it hard

Nobody argues against this; they just schedule it later. The structure has to be a requirement of the AI feature itself, something the feature cannot ship without, or it becomes the accessibility team's problem after the fact. It also has to be real structure, exposed to assistive technology, not visual structure painted onto the player or the viewer.

## When it fails

An index that is only a row of thumbnails, a marker that is only a graphic, an excerpt with no text behind it: each one is an AI feature that quietly rebuilt the black box it was meant to open.

## In my work

I like designing for as many senses as possible: operating a video with the keyboard alone, making sense of one with no sound, or making sense of one with no picture. Designing from a new angle is a fresh way of living. When we open our thinking with that kind of inclusiveness, we find something simple: the best accessibility design is the best design for everyone. In the video shopping work on this site, that was where we started.
