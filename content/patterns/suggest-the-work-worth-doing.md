## What users needed

Once a video could be made in minutes, the sellers' question changed. It was no longer "how do I make a video" but "which of my two hundred products deserves one." They did not want to make more videos. They wanted to make the right ones, and they had no way to tell which those were without an analyst.

## The problem

Generative tools lower the cost of producing a thing to almost nothing, and the bottleneck moves upstream to the decision of what to produce. A tool that opens on "what would you like to make" leaves that decision with the person, who has no more information than before. The result is either paralysis or volume: nothing gets made, or everything does, and neither is a good use of the person's time.

## The pattern

Put the opportunities first. Before the creation screen, show a short ranked list of what is worth doing, drawn from the person's own data: the product that gets visits but few purchases, the one whose only video is old, the category where similar products with video sell better. Each item says why it is on the list, in a sentence and a small chart, and carries one action that starts the work with that item already filled in.

The person's first decision is which opportunity to take, not how to begin.

## What makes it hard

The ranking has to rest on signals the person recognizes as theirs. A recommendation that cannot be explained in terms of their own numbers reads as the system's agenda, not their opportunity. The explanation must also be proportionate: enough to decide, not a report. And the list must stay short; ten ranked opportunities are a to-do list, three are a choice.

## When it fails

If the ranking is wrong, the person spends effort on the wrong product and the tool has cost them more than a blank page would. If the reasons are generic ("this product could benefit from video"), nobody believes the ranking at all. And if an opportunity is stated as a promise ("this will lift sales by 14 percent"), the first miss ends trust in every later suggestion; it should be stated as a reason, not a forecast.

## In my work

The management home of the AI video creation tool for Amazon sellers opens with recommended products, each with the suggested kind of video, the reason it was chosen and a single "Create video" action. The reasons draw on that product's sales trend, how its existing videos are watched and converted, and how similar products perform. Choosing one takes the seller to a creation page that is already filled in. The metrics overview and the video library sit on the same page, so choosing what to make next and seeing what the last one did are one screen apart.
