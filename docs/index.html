What a shopper wants from a video is not to watch it. It is to ask it.

Design patterns for AI products, written down from products I have designed: markers you can tap, indexes you can jump through, answers you can check, and the structure underneath that makes a video work for everyone.

Patterns

8 so far, all from video shopping. Each shows which stage of the flow it serves: ask, answer, check or decide.

  1. Ask, answer, check, decide

    Design the whole chain from a shopper's question to their decision as one flow. Break any link and trust in the assistant is hollowed out.

  3. Index by product

    Turn a long video into a table of contents organized by the products in it, so a shopper can jump to the one they care about and skip the rest.

  4. Index by question

    Organize a video by the questions a shopper actually has, not by the products or the creator's edit, and hand the video back to the viewer's own priorities.

  5. Show the seconds that answer

    When an AI answers with a clip, give the shopper the context to open it, keep watching and check that those seconds really say what the AI claims.

    Decide, as a design matter, when the AI should answer with confidence and when it should admit it has nothing, because trust lives on the line between the two.

  8. Structure is accessibility

    The structure AI adds to a video so it can be asked is the same structure that makes it work without sound, without pictures or without a mouse. Build it as the foundation, not a patch.
