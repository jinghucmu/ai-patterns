Close the loop on results

Put what the AI made under the same measures as everything else, next to the work, and let the result lead to the next decision, so the person can tell whether the AI is worth using and what to do next.

From
AI video creation for sellers, Amazon Seller Central
Themes
  • AI that does the work
Added
November 3, 2026

Where it sits in the flow

  1. AskThe person has a question, a thing they want to find, or a task they want done.
  2. AnswerThe AI returns something: an excerpt, a marker, a generated answer, or a prepared plan.
  3. CheckThe person looks at the evidence and decides whether to believe it.
  4. DecideThe person acts: proceeds, moves on, or corrects the AI.
  5. DoThe AI carries the task through on the person's behalf, and shows what it did.

What users needed

The sellers who kept using the tool were the ones who had found the metrics page, and most of them had found it by accident. Seeing that a video had sold something was what made them make the next one. The others had no idea whether their videos did anything, and a tool whose results are invisible is a tool that gets tried once.

The problem

Generative tools measure themselves by output: how many things were made, how fast. The person measures them by outcome: did it work. When the outcome lives somewhere else, in a report nobody opens or a dashboard that does not distinguish the AI's work from the rest, the person cannot answer the only question that decides whether they continue. And without the result, the next decision is a guess again, so the tool never gets better at helping them.

The pattern

Show the results of what the AI made in the same place as the work and on the same measures as everything else: the views, the conversions, the orders, whatever the person already uses to judge their work. Separate the AI-made items from the rest so the comparison is visible, and let the comparison go either way; the AI's line may sit below the other one and the chart should say so plainly. Then connect the result to the next action: break it down by variant, make the next one, change the strategy. The loop closes when a result becomes a decision.

What makes it hard

The comparison has to be honest about what it is. AI-made and hand-made items differ in product, age, traffic and timing, so a gap between the two lines is an observation, not a proof. The design must state that without burying the comparison in caveats. The measures have to be the person's own, not new ones invented for the AI. And the results have to be where the person already looks; a results page that must be found is a results page that will not be.

When it fails

If the chart only ever shows the AI winning, people stop believing it, and they should. If the comparison is read as causal ("AI videos convert 30 percent better"), the person makes decisions on a correlation. If the results are a month late, the loop is too slow to steer by. And if the data is shown without a next step, the person has a report where they needed a decision.

In my work

In the AI video creation tool for Amazon sellers, the analytics page compares AI-made and other videos on uploads, views, attributed sales and conversion rate over time, with the two lines free to cross. The assistant explains what the comparison can and cannot show, and offers to break the result down by colour variant or to plan the next video from it. The management home carries the same summary at the top, so the loop from result to next decision is on the page the seller starts from.

Related patterns

  • Suggest the work worth doingWhen making things becomes cheap, choosing what to make becomes the hard part. Let the AI rank the opportunities from the person's own data and explain each one, so the first decision is which task, not how to start.
  • Help inside the work, not beside itPut the assistant inside the task, with the task's state in front of it, so it explains, adjusts and recovers in context. Never make the person leave their work, or redo what they could do faster themselves, to use the AI.
  • Show the evidence behind the answerWhen an AI answers from a source, return the source with the answer, so the person can open it, read on, and check that it really says what the AI claims.
