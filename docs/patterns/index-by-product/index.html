Index by product

Turn a long video into a table of contents organized by the products in it, so a shopper can jump to the one they care about and skip the rest.

From
Product Jumplinks, Amazon video shopping

Where it sits in the flow

  1. AskThe person has a question, or a thing they want to find.
  2. AnswerThe AI returns something: a clip, a marker, a segment, a generated answer.
  3. CheckThe person looks at the evidence and decides whether to believe it.
  4. DecideThe person acts: buys, moves on, or corrects the AI.

The problem

A thirty-minute "cat bed showdown" compares six beds. The shopper is only deciding between two of them. The scrubber does not know where any bed begins or ends, so the shopper drags it back and forth hoping to land on the right one, or gives up and watches all thirty minutes.

The pattern

Let AI split the video into one segment per product and show that list as the video's table of contents. The shopper taps the bed they care about and lands at the point where the creator starts talking about it. The other twenty-five minutes are still there, but nobody has to sit through them.

What makes it hard

Recognizing which product is on screen is the easy part. The hard part is the real rhythm of how people edit: a creator hops between products, comes back to compare two of them side by side, drops in an unrelated aside, then returns to the first product for a final verdict. A clean table of contents has to be built on top of that mess.

So the design question is not "how do we segment correctly" but "what happens when we don't". An automatically generated table of contents has to fail gracefully. The shopper should notice a wrong segment quickly, and should have an easy way to push back. That part is its own pattern: Make mistakes easy to catch.

When it fails

A segment that starts in the wrong place sends the shopper to the wrong bed. If there is no obvious way to see that it is wrong, they assume the creator never covered what they wanted and leave. The index gets blamed for a gap it created.

The accessibility dividend

A product index is a text outline of a video. Someone using a screen reader can hear the six beds and choose one before any video plays. Someone on a slow connection can decide what to load. See Structure is accessibility.

In my work

Product Jumplinks give Amazon shopping videos a product-by-product table of contents. I designed the index and how it behaves when the AI's segmentation does not match the video.

Related patterns

  • Index by questionOrganize a video by the questions a shopper actually has, not by the products or the creator's edit, and hand the video back to the viewer's own priorities.
  • Tap what you seePut the action inside the frame, at the second the thing appears, so a video turns from display into something you can touch.
  • Make mistakes easy to catchAssume the AI will be wrong some of the time, and design so the shopper notices quickly and can push back with almost no effort.
