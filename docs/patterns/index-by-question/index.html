Index by question

Organize content by the questions a person actually has, not by the things in it or the order its author chose, and hand the content back to the reader's own priorities.

From
Topic Jumplinks, Amazon video shopping

Added
October 10, 2026

Where it sits in the flow

  1. AskThe person has a question, or a thing they want to find.
  2. AnswerThe AI returns something: an excerpt, a marker, a segment, a generated answer.
  3. CheckThe person looks at the evidence and decides whether to believe it.
  4. DecideThe person acts: proceeds, moves on, or corrects the AI.

What users needed

People's questions were about their own situation, not about the names of things: is it hard to use, what are the side effects, how much does it hold. They needed to get to the part that answered their own question, and they were impatient with any path through the content that was organized around the author's priorities instead of theirs.

The problem

Someone choosing between options is not really asking "which one". They are asking whether it is hard to use, whether it has a problem they have heard about, whether it is enough for their case, what the result is like, and what the upkeep is. A long piece of content answers all of those, but scattered across thirty minutes or forty pages in the order the author happened to go through them.

The pattern

Let AI organize the content by topic instead of by thing: ease of use, known problems, capacity, results, upkeep. Each topic becomes a place the person can jump to. The content is still the author's, but the path through it belongs to the reader.

This is the pattern I like most, because it comes closest to the person who came to a thirty-minute piece wanting only the three seconds that answer their question.

What makes it hard

Index by what it covers leans on recognizing things. This leans on something much harder: understanding what is being said and shown well enough to know that a stretch of content is about upkeep, even when nobody uses the word. The topics have to be the reader's words, not the model's, and the set of topics has to be short enough to scan and complete enough to trust.

When it fails

A topic that is named in jargon, or that is really two topics, stops being a shortcut. Too many topics and the index is as long as the content. A topic that lands in the wrong place costs trust in all the others, so the same correction path matters here as in the index by what it covers.

The accessibility dividend

A topic index is the closest thing a recording has to headings. It lets a screen-reader user, a keyboard user or someone with limited attention move through a recording the way they already move through a document. See Structure is accessibility.

In my work

Topic Jumplinks organize Amazon shopping videos by the questions people bring to them: for an air fryer review, how hard it is to use, whether it smokes, how much it holds, what the food comes out like, how annoying it is to clean. I designed how the topics are presented and how a person moves between them.

Related patterns

  • Index by what it coversTurn a long piece of content into a table of contents organized by the things in it, so a person can jump to the one they care about and skip the rest.
  • Show the evidence behind the answerWhen an AI answers from a source, return the source with the answer, so the person can open it, read on, and check that it really says what the AI claims.
  • Structure is accessibilityThe structure AI adds to content so it can be asked is the same structure that makes it work without sound, without pictures or without a mouse. Build it as the foundation, not a patch.
