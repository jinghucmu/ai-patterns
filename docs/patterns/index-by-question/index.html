Index by question

Organize a video by the questions a shopper actually has, not by the products or the creator's edit, and hand the video back to the viewer's own priorities.

Added

Where it sits in the flow

  1. AskThe person has a question, or a thing they want to find.
  2. AnswerThe AI returns something: a clip, a marker, a segment, a generated answer.
  3. CheckThe person looks at the evidence and decides whether to believe it.
  4. DecideThe person acts: buys, moves on, or corrects the AI.

The problem

Someone choosing an air fryer is not really asking "which air fryer". They are asking whether it is hard to use, whether it smokes, whether it holds enough food, what the food comes out like, and how annoying it is to clean. A review answers all of those, but scattered across thirty minutes in the order the creator happened to cook things.

The pattern

Let AI organize the video by topic instead of by product: operating difficulty, smoke, capacity, food results, cleaning. Each topic becomes a place the shopper can jump to. The video is still the creator's, but the path through it belongs to the shopper.

This is the pattern I like most, because it comes closest to the person who scrolled into a thirty-minute review wanting only the three seconds when the wings come out of the basket.

What makes it hard

Index by product leans on object recognition. This leans on something much harder: understanding what is being said and shown well enough to know that a stretch of video is about cleaning, even when nobody says the word. The topics have to be the shopper's words, not the model's, and the set of topics has to be short enough to scan and complete enough to trust.

When it fails

A topic that is named in jargon, or that is really two topics, stops being a shortcut. Too many topics and the index is as long as the video. A topic that lands on the wrong seconds costs trust in all the others, so the same correction path matters here as in the product index.

The accessibility dividend

A topic index is the closest thing a video has to headings. It lets a screen-reader user, a keyboard user or someone with limited attention move through a video the way they already move through a document. See Structure is accessibility.

In my work

Topic Jumplinks organize Amazon shopping videos by the questions shoppers bring to them. I designed how the topics are presented and how a shopper moves between them.

Related patterns

  • Index by productTurn a long video into a table of contents organized by the products in it, so a shopper can jump to the one they care about and skip the rest.
  • Show the seconds that answerWhen an AI answers with a clip, give the shopper the context to open it, keep watching and check that those seconds really say what the AI claims.
  • Structure is accessibilityThe structure AI adds to a video so it can be asked is the same structure that makes it work without sound, without pictures or without a mouse. Build it as the foundation, not a patch.
