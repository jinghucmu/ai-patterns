8 patterns, each from a product I designed. Filter by theme, or read them in order.

  1. Ask, answer, check, decide

    Design the whole chain from a person's question to their decision as one flow. Break any link and trust in the AI is hollowed out.

    From Multimodal Alexa

    1. Ask
    2. Answer
    3. Check
    4. Decide

  2. Act where it appears

    When AI recognizes something inside a piece of content, put the action on that thing, at the moment it appears, so the content turns from display into something you can act on.

    From Video Hotspots

    1. Ask
    2. Answer

  3. Index by what it covers

    Turn a long piece of content into a table of contents organized by the things in it, so a person can jump to the one they care about and skip the rest.

    From Product Jumplinks

  4. Index by question

    Organize content by the questions a person actually has, not by the things in it or the order its author chose, and hand the content back to the reader's own priorities.

    From Topic Jumplinks

  5. Show the evidence behind the answer

    When an AI answers from a source, return the source with the answer, so the person can open it, read on, and check that it really says what the AI claims.

    From Multimodal Alexa, clip answers

  6. Say "I don't know"

    Decide, as a design matter, when the AI should answer with confidence and when it should admit it has nothing, because trust lives on the line between the two.

    From Multimodal Alexa, generated answers

  7. Make mistakes easy to catch

    Assume the AI will be wrong some of the time, and design so the person notices quickly and can push back with almost no effort.

    From Product Jumplinks, Topic Jumplinks

  8. Structure is accessibility

    The structure AI adds to content so it can be asked is the same structure that makes it work without sound, without pictures or without a mouse. Build it as the foundation, not a patch.
